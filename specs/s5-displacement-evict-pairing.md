## Problem Statement

Same-id displacement ends with a slot eviction that the caller must remember to perform. The enqueue funnel displaces the old row through one table-owned exchange, then unconditionally evicts a stale slot waiter under the same task id. The pairing between those two steps lives only in caller discipline: nothing in the exchange result forces the evict, and the evict fires even when no displacement happened.

That leaves two failure modes behind one call site. Forgetting the evict strands a stale waiter in the slot queue, where it blocks the FIFO head and starves every waiter behind it until timeout or cancel. Firing the evict without a displacement evicts a waiter the caller never displaced — a same-id waiter owned by someone else — resuming it as refused even though its row is still live. The slot module itself is innocent: it owns a correct stale-vs-self eviction mechanism, but Tasks crosses it from the wrong depth, spelling the intent at the call site instead of draining an obligation the exchange produced.

## Solution

Make displacement-to-evict unforgetable without touching the slot contract. The table-owned exchange result carries the evict obligation (present exactly when a non-terminal occupant was displaced), and a Tasks-owned drain step pairs exchange-plus-evict so no caller can take one half without the other. The evict fires only when the obligation is present; the unconditional stale-evict on every enqueue goes away. Lock order is preserved — row lock first, slot lock after, never nested — and waiter delivery plus park-waiter cancellation stay outside both locks exactly as today. Observable behavior through the scheduling facade is unchanged except the stray-evict removal, which is the bug this candidate closes.

## User Stories

1. As a Tasks maintainer, I want one paired displacement-to-evict step, so that I stop auditing whether every exchange caller remembered its stale-evict.
2. As a concurrent scheduler, I want the evict obligation carried by the exchange result, so that a displacement without a follow-up evict is unrepresentable in review.
3. As a same-id reuser with no displacement (fresh id or sequential reuse after terminal), I want no slot evict to fire, so that my queued waiter is never resumed as refused by someone else's enqueue.
4. As a displaced lease, I want exactly-once terminal delivery preserved, so that my completion waiters still resume expired exactly once regardless of the pairing move.
5. As a stale slot waiter, I want eviction only after my lease already terminalized, so that my false-resume always lands on an already-terminal lease and never reads as a live admission refusal.
6. As a surviving waiter (the newcomer's own queued wait), I want the stale match to keep sparing my owner, so that displacement never yanks the lease that just took the id.
7. As a FIFO waiter behind a stale entry, I want the stale entry drained on every displacement, so that no stranded head blocks my admission after the holder releases.
8. As a slot operator, I want the eviction primitive untouched (stale vs self, first-only, resume-false outside the lock), so that every existing slot guarantee survives this change byte-for-byte.
9. As a lock-order reviewer, I want row lock first then slot lock with no nesting, so that the established lock discipline stays provably intact.
10. As a cancel caller, I want cancel-while-waiting still routed through the self intent only, so that cancelling one lease never yanks another holder's wait.
11. As a park reviewer, I want displaced park state (waiting flag plus waiter task) still taken in the exchange and cancelled exactly once outside the lock, so that no parked waiter strands when its row is displaced.
12. As a schedule-and-wait caller, I want the waiter-attach identity guard unchanged, so that a mismatched row still provably means my lease already settled.
13. As a test author, I want pairing proven by behavior through the public schedule seam, so that the evict obligation is verified by outcomes (stale gone, newcomer admitted, no stray evict) not by helper spelling — except one mechanical check proving no bare exchange without an evict drain remains in the funnel.
14. As a future contributor, I want the only way to displace to also carry the evict, so that no second exchange-without-evict protocol can be reintroduced by a new path.
15. As an ADR reader, I want the change traceable to single-settlement, unified-lifecycle-ownership, single scoped-hold, Lease-keyed hold, and shared-slot-domain decisions, so that the pairing clearly respects prior decisions rather than reopening them.

## Implementation Decisions

- **Module**: Tasks remains the deep module behind the schedule/cancel/pending/active seam. The lifecycle table keeps owning rows, order, and counts; the slot controller keeps owning admission, FIFO wake order, cancellable wait, scope-exit release, and the single waiter-eviction mechanism. No new module is introduced; the pairing lives in Tasks as the policy owner.
- **Interface**: the exchange result gains the evict obligation alongside the displaced delivery payload (displaced lease, its completion waiters, its park waiter). The obligation is present exactly when a non-terminal occupant was displaced and absent otherwise (fresh id, terminal occupant, identity match). A Tasks-owned drain step consumes the obligation: when present it calls the existing stale-evict intent with the newcomer identity; when absent it calls nothing. The unconditional stale-evict on every enqueue is deleted.
- **Obligation shape**: an optional descriptor naming the displaced task id plus the newcomer owner is sufficient; exact spelling at implementation time. The rule is presence-equals-displaced, so reviewers audit one invariant instead of one call site. Either an obligation-carrying result or a helper that performs exchange-plus-evict as one funnel step satisfies the goal; carrying the obligation in the result is preferred because it keeps the table as the single source of displaced-vs-fresh truth while Tasks owns the drain.
- **Stale vs self**: displacement drains through the stale intent only (evict waiter under the task id whose owner differs from the newcomer). The self intent stays reserved for cancellable-wait cancellation and scope-exit paths. No intent is added, renamed, or re-keyed; the slot predicate is untouched.
- **Ordering**: settled-plus-inserted atomically, then evicted, with waiter delivery and park-waiter cancellation outside both locks. Settle-first stays load-bearing: the stale resume-false must land on an already-terminal lease. Delivery may precede or follow the evict (neither holds a lock) but the evict must never precede the exchange and never nest inside the row lock.
- **Lock discipline**: the exchange holds the one scheduler lock once and returns; the drain takes the slot lock after, never inside. Waiter delivery, waiter-task cancellation, expiry race, registry wait, and slot hold stay outside the locked sections exactly as today. No lock-nesting in either direction.
- **Leak closure**: the stranded-head hazard is closed by construction — every displacement yields an obligation and the funnel drains every obligation. A forgotten drain is review-visible (unused obligation) rather than silent (missing call). The FIFO head can no longer be a displaced waiter after its row is gone.
- **Stray-evict closure**: gating the evict on the obligation removes the wrong-module evict where an enqueue with no displacement resumed someone else's waiter as refused. This is the sole intended observable-behavior delta; all other facade outcomes (overwrite expires old exactly once, sequential reuse works, FIFO order, counts snapshots, retry identity, schedule-wait guard) are identical.
- **Caller-discipline argument**: the funnel is single today, but the exchange is a table method any future Tasks path can call. Discipline ("remember the evict") is a comment; obligation ("the result says evict") is a type-adjacent fact the compiler and the reviewer can see. The pairing moves the rule from the caller comment into the result shape, so the next path inherits it.
- **Slot contract untouched**: the eviction primitive keeps first-only removal, false-resume outside the lock, self-retire and stranger no-ops, admitted-holder immunity, and the Lease-keyed hold spelling with the newcomer-keyed stale exception. No Lease, row, or lifecycle logic enters the controller; no admission, release, or install logic enters Tasks.
- **Seam**: the highest seam stays the scheduling facade plus the single result rendezvous and the registry whole-set wait. Tests cross that seam; no new seam is created. The lifecycle table remains the single row writer; the controller remains the single slot writer.
- **Depth**: depth decreases by one caller-visible pairing rule (two steps to remember become one result to drain). No new abstraction layer is added; the obligation is the same displaced truth the result already carries, extended with the evict half.
- **Coordination**: lands on the post-exchange post-state (same-id exchange fused, waiter-eviction fused). Shares the enqueue funnel with the sibling enqueue-touching candidate — sequence the two so only one moves the funnel at a time and the second rebases onto the paired drain rather than the unpaired call site.
- **Respects ADRs**: single-expiry-clock plus task-keyed slots (eviction touches only queued waiters, never held entries); scoped-hold FIFO-cancellable ownership plus single scoped seam (admission, order, cancellable wait, scope-exit release untouched); Lease-keyed hold with the newcomer-keyed stale exception preserved; shared slot domain across generations (evict still crosses the shared domain, never a per-generation copy); Store-to-Tasks lock order (never nested).

## Testing Decisions

- A good test crosses the public Tasks seam and asserts externally observable behavior (displacement evicts the stale waiter and admits the newcomer; enqueue without displacement evicts nothing; FIFO order and counts agree; overwrite still expires old exactly once with new completing; sequential reuse after terminal inserts cleanly), never the spelling of the obligation or the drain helper — except one mechanical check proving no bare exchange without an evict drain remains in the funnel and no unconditional stale-evict remains on the non-displaced path.
- Modules under test: the exchange-plus-drain pairing (obligation present exactly on non-terminal displacement, absent on fresh id and terminal reuse), the enqueue funnel (stale drained once per displacement, zero evicts otherwise, settle-first ordering intact), and the untouched slot primitive (stale evicts, self cancels, self-retire no-ops, stranger untouched — existing pins only, no re-pinning).
- Prior art: same-id exchange pins (concurrent overwrite expires old once with new completing, overwrite under slot contention keeps the new waiter, displaced parked waiter cancelled once and never runs, cancel-after-overwrite lands on the new row, schedule-wait guard never joins the new lease), slot-hold suites (double-acquire rejection, stale-evicts plus self-evicts plus no-op guards, FIFO wake), lifecycle suites (terminal removal plus counts, double-park plus cancel-while-parked), determinism suites (virtual-time timeout, cancel-pins-expired, priority high-before-low, slot serialization). Every one stays green with zero test-semantics edits except new pairing pins added beside them.
- Verification per ticket: `swift build` green plus focused settlement/slot/same-id suites first, then full `swift test` green; mechanical check shows the obligation drained on every exchange path and the unconditional evict gone.

## Out of Scope

- Any change to lifecycle reachability, priority ordering, retry budget or void-completion policy, waiter preservation or exactly-once delivery semantics, slot limits or release ownership (stays with activation scope-exit), expiry resolution, or time adapters.
- Changing the public Lease shape, the Bridge live-view reflection, State creation/observation, registry emission order or lock discipline, generation prune or shared-slot coupling, or deterministic-time spelling.
- Touching the slot eviction primitive itself, the atomic install, Deadline internals, the registry wait implementation, or the activation gate beyond its existing call site.
- Reopening single-settlement, unified-lifecycle-ownership, scoped-hold, Lease-keyed hold, or same-id policy decisions: no revisit trigger is met; the pairing extends them to evict atomicity of intent, it does not redesign them.
- Reformatting or renames beyond the obligation plus drain.

## Further Notes

- Self-grill record (autonomous, no questions asked; recommendations adopted):
  - Q1 — Obligation in result vs helper pairing exchange-plus-evict, or both? Adopted both-as-one: result carries the obligation (present iff displaced), Tasks-owned drain consumes it. Pure-helper without the obligation reintroduces discipline (caller must remember the helper); pure-obligation without the drain leaves consumption undisciplined. Together the type states the debt and the funnel pays it.
  - Q2 — Lock order: row first, slot after, never nested — where does the drain sit? Adopted strictly after the row lock releases, beside waiter delivery and park-waiter cancellation, all outside both locks. Nesting the slot evict inside the exchange would fuse lock domains and violate the Store-to-Tasks order; queuing the evict before the exchange would evict before settle-first holds.
  - Q3 — Stale vs self: why is displacement always stale, never self? Adopted stale-only because the newcomer names a different holder than any queued waiter under the id; self would evict the newcomer's own wait (or no-op) and leave the stale head stranded. Self stays reserved for cancellation paths where the canceller names its own wait.
  - Q4 — Leak hazard concreteness: what exactly strands? Adopted FIFO-head account: only the head is ever woken on release, so a stale waiter parked at the head pins every waiter behind it; the stale continuation also leaks until its own timeout or cancel. The pairing closes the head-strand, not just the continuation leak.
  - Q5 — Why does caller discipline fail when the funnel is single today? Adopted future-caller account: the exchange is callable from any future Tasks path, and nothing in its result names the evict debt, so the next path can displace without evicting and no compiler or grep rule fires. The obligation makes the debt visible at the type/review level instead of the comment level.
  - Q6 — Does gating the evict on displacement change behavior? Adopted yes, intentionally, in exactly one way: the stray-evict on non-displaced enqueue disappears. All other facade behavior is preserved. The stray-evict is classified as the wrong-module bug this candidate exists to close, proven by a no-displacement-evicts-nothing pin.
  - Rejected: fusing the slot evict into the table transition (nests locks), adding a new eviction intent (slot contract churn for zero leverage), keeping the unconditional evict as harmless (it is not — same-id waiters from unrelated modules share the id key).
- Deletion test is the acceptance bar: deleting the unconditional stale-evict call and routing every displacement through the obligation drain changes nothing observable through the public seam except stray evicts stop firing.
- Source of prototype decisions: none — no prototype; decisions derive from the exchange-plus-unconditional-evict inventory and the FIFO-head leak account.
