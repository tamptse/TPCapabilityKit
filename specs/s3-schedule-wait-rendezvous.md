## Problem Statement

The schedule-and-wait entry reaches its result through two separate locked crossings with a continuation allocation wedged between them: the enqueue funnel first inserts the new pending row (displacing any non-terminal occupant for the same identity), then a second crossing attaches the caller's continuation waiter to that row — with an identity guard that detects a settled-or-replaced row and resumes immediately instead. A reviewer trying to answer "how does a waiting caller meet its outcome?" must hold both crossings plus the wedged gap together: between insert and attach, a cancel or a same-identity reschedule can terminalize the fresh row while its waiter is still homeless, and only the second crossing's guard keeps that waiter from hanging or joining another lease's delivery list. The guard is correct, but it is a patch over a window the entry itself creates. No behavior bug is alleged — cancel-while-waiting delivers exactly once and displacement settles the old waiter first — but the schedule-and-wait rendezvous carries the same hidden depth the park fuse and the same-identity exchange already removed from their paths: one rendezvous reviewed as two crossings with no named seam.

## Solution

Give the rendezvous one table-owned seam: the lifecycle table parks the continuation waiter as part of the same locked transition that inserts the new row, or immediately returns the already-settled lease when the identity check finds the row gone or owned by another lease. The schedule-and-wait entry makes one call; the table decides park vs resume-now in the same transition and callers only deliver (resume the continuation, deliver taken waiters, cancel the taken park waiter) outside the lock. The park capability-waiter system stays untouched. No behavior change except atomicity: the insert-to-attach window closes, and the identity guard moves from caller patch to table-owned outcome.

## User Stories

1. As a Tasks maintainer, I want enqueue plus continuation-attach to cross one table seam, so that I stop auditing two lock crossings plus a wedged gap to answer one rendezvous question.
2. As a waiting caller, I want my continuation parked in the same transition that inserts my row, so that no cancel or reschedule racing the gap can ever find my waiter homeless.
3. As a cancelled waiter, I want cancel racing my attach to deliver me exactly once through the normal row path, so that the outcome no longer depends on which side of the gap the cancel lands.
4. As a displaced lease, I want same-identity displacement of my in-flight wait to settle me first with my waiter delivered exactly once, so that I resume with expiry rather than hanging or joining the new lease.
5. As a new lease sharing a displaced identity, I want a clean pending row carrying only my own continuation waiter, so that the old lease's waiters never leak into my delivery list.
6. As a cancel caller, I want cancel-before-attach and cancel-after-attach to both resolve through one row, so that no cancel is lost across the fused seam.
7. As a retry integrator, I want the parked continuation to ride the row through retry re-queue untouched, so that flaky-then-recovered waits still resume with the recovered value exactly as today.
8. As a slot operator, I want stale-evict-after-settle ordering and owner-scoped matching untouched, so that the new lease's queued waiter survives while the old lease's stale waiter is removed exactly as today.
9. As a park reviewer, I want the capability-wait task system (park flag plus waiter task, wake-clear, terminal-take-and-cancel) untouched, so that the park single-seam fuse needs no re-audit.
10. As a settlement reviewer, I want the single terminal decision plus waiter preservation plus scope-exit release untouched, so that this candidate extends single-settlement instead of reopening it.
11. As a test author, I want the rendezvous proven by behavior through the public schedule/cancel/wait seam, so that the fuse is verified by outcomes not by seam spelling — except one mechanical check proving the standalone attach-after-enqueue path is gone from the entry.
12. As a future contributor, I want one rendezvous entry to call instead of two, so that no second attach protocol can be re-introduced by a new wait style.
13. As an ADR reader, I want the change traceable to single-settlement, unified lifecycle ownership, single rendezvous with outcome preservation, and same-identity policy decisions, so that the fuse clearly respects prior decisions rather than reopening them.
14. As a Bridge consumer, I want wait-then-run delivery and timeout behavior identical before and after, so that this refactor stays invisible from Objective-C.

## Implementation Decisions

- **Module**: Tasks remains the deep module behind the schedule/cancel/pending/active seam. The lifecycle table gains one rendezvous transition (new-lease intent plus execution plus continuation waiter in, parked vs already-settled out); the schedule-and-wait entry keeps its result-shaping (completed resumes with the typed value, every other terminal resumes nil) as the only outcome reader. No new module is introduced.
- **Interface**: the public scheduling seam is unchanged (schedule/schedule-and-wait/cancel/pending/active plus the result rendezvous). The internal change is insert plus waiter-attach collapse into one table-owned seam: the entry passes the new lease with its execution and its continuation waiter together, the table atomically displaces any non-terminal occupant for the same identity and installs the new pending row already carrying the waiter, returning the displaced delivery payload for outside-lock handling exactly as the exchange does today.
- **Rendezvous outcome shape**: the fused transition returns one of two outcomes in the same locked step — parked (waiter stored on the new row; entry then pumps the pending path) or settled-early (the identity check found no row or a row owned by another lease, or the fresh lease already terminal; entry resumes immediately with the returned settled lease and never attaches elsewhere). The settled-early branch stays as a safety net even though fusion closes the gap that exercises it today: it is the proof that append-to-wrong-row is impossible for any future caller, mirroring how the same-identity exchange kept its guard as safety net rather than primary mechanism.
- **Settlement ownership**: the single terminal decision stays the only decider and is not edited. The fused transition reuses the existing displacement terminalization (displaced lease terminalizes to expired together with its row removal inside the one locked step) and the existing terminal take (waiters plus park waiter taken with row removal, delivered and cancelled outside the lock). No second terminal path and no new delivery path are introduced.
- **Evict ordering**: settled-then-evicted-then-inserted observable order is preserved as today. The slot stale-evict stays a separate lock step after the table transition under its own lock with the same owner-scoped stale match, never nested — the fused seam changes what enters the row atomically, not what happens after it.
- **Park interplay**: the capability-wait task system is explicitly out of the fused seam. Parked-state take-and-cancel on displacement, wake-clear, and terminal-take-and-cancel keep the park single-seam vocabulary verbatim; the continuation waiter joins the row waiter list, never the park waiter slot, so the two waiter systems still meet only in the existing terminal and displacement takes.
- **Retry**: retry re-queue through the same lease identity is untouched. Because the continuation waiter is present from row birth, the existing retry transition (waiters preserved in place, execution optionally refreshed, row re-queued) carries it with zero special-casing; waiter preservation across retry falls out of birth-attach rather than needing a second rule.
- **Depth**: depth decreases by one caller-visible step (two crossings to one). No new abstraction is added; the fused seam is the same row write plus lease liveness check already owned by the table, with the waiter store moved adjacent, not a new rendezvous layer.
- **Seam**: the highest seam stays the Tasks schedule/cancel/pending/active facade plus the single result rendezvous and the registry whole-set wait. Tests cross that seam; no new seam is created. The lifecycle table remains the single writer: each lease mutation happens together with its row write in one locked transition.
- **Expand–contract**: add the fused rendezvous seam beside the existing enqueue-then-attach spelling, migrate the single schedule-and-wait entry, then delete the standalone attach path from the entry. CI stays green batch to batch because the old form still exists until the migrate ticket lands.
- **Lock discipline**: the fused transition holds the one scheduler lock once and never nests. The continuation waiter closure must not suspend under lock; continuation resume, waiter delivery, park-waiter cancellation, slot evict, expiry race, registry wait, and slot hold stay outside the locked section exactly as today.
- **Locality**: insert plus waiter-attach plus the identity check share one reviewable block with the single transition, so the schedule-and-wait rendezvous needs no hop into the entry comment to audit.
- **Leverage**: leverage is window closure plus guard relocation: one crossing to audit, one owner of waiter lifetime from birth, zero caller-side settled-early patch to keep in sync. Risk is bounded because the move is spelling-plus-ordering only — same rows, same places, same snapshot derivation, same waiter identity and delivery — only the interleaving window closes.
- **S1 sequencing**: this candidate sequences after the enqueue-seam work it overlaps (the schedule-and-wait entry's funnel shape is owned there first). The fused rendezvous transition is written against that post-state and extends its seam with waiter-attach rather than re-spelling the funnel; see Further Notes.
- **Respects ADRs**: single-settlement path constrains outcome, delivery, and release to stay untouched; unified lifecycle ownership requires the table (not the entry) to own the row transition plus waiter lifetime; single rendezvous with outcome preservation requires every wait to stay on the one identity-guarded path with waiter preservation across retry; same-identity policy stays (displacement settles old to expired, new takes identity; sequential reuse after terminal keeps working).

## Testing Decisions

- A good test crosses the public Tasks seam (schedule/schedule-and-wait/cancel/pending/active) and asserts externally observable behavior (wait resumes with the value on success and nil on every other terminal; cancel of a pending wait delivers nil exactly once; same-identity displacement of an in-flight wait expires the old waiter exactly once while the new lease completes; retry preserves the waiter to recovery; counts drain to zero), never the spelling of the fused seam — except one mechanical check proving no standalone attach-after-enqueue protocol remains in the entry.
- Modules under test: lifecycle table rendezvous transition (fresh identity parks the waiter; terminal-or-missing row returns settled-early; mismatched row identity returns the settled lease without appending elsewhere; displaced payload taken for outside delivery), schedule-and-wait entry (park pumps the pending path; settled-early resumes immediately without pumping).
- Prior art: settlement suites (waiter preserved across retry, completed/failed exactly-once, same-identity overwrite expires the old row once with the new row completing, sequential reuse delivering both completions), guard suites (cancel of pending wait delivers nil once, void-with-retries reuses identity, schedule delivers same identity once), scheduler suites (retry recovery through the facade, cancel-while-parked landing terminal), determinism suites (virtual-time timeout, cancel-pins-expired, priority order, slot serialization), bridge delivery suites (wait-then-run arrival on the given queue). The fuse must keep every one green with zero test-semantics edits.
- Verification per ticket: build green plus full test suite green; the standalone attach-after-enqueue caller protocol shrinks monotonically (present beside the new seam after fuse, absent after migrate, unreferenced after pin).

## Out of Scope

- Any change to lifecycle reachability, priority ordering, retry budget or void-completion policy, waiter preservation or exactly-once delivery semantics, slot limits or release ownership (stays with activation scope-exit), expiry resolution (task timeout vs configured default), or time adapters.
- Changing the public lease shape, the typed-optional wait signatures, the Bridge live-view reflection, state creation/observation, registry emission order or lock discipline, generation prune or shared-slot coupling, or deterministic-time spelling.
- Touching the park capability-waiter primitive, the slot evict primitive, deadline internals, the registry wait implementation, or the activation gate beyond its existing call site.
- Reopening single-settlement, unified-lifecycle-ownership, single-rendezvous, scoped-hold, or same-identity policy decisions: no revisit trigger is met; the fuse extends them to the schedule-and-wait entry, it does not redesign them.
- Reformatting or renames beyond the fuse.

## Further Notes

- **Blocked by S1 conceptually**: the enqueue funnel both candidates touch is sequenced first under S1; this candidate's first ticket starts only after S1's funnel shape lands, and is written against that post-state. No ticket here re-spells the funnel independently.
- Grill verdict (autonomous self-grill; decided, no questions asked):
  - S3-Q1 Two waiter systems (row waiters vs park waiter) — does the fused seam touch the park waiter? Adopted: no. The continuation waiter joins the row waiter list; park flag plus waiter task, wake-clear, and terminal-take-and-cancel stay verbatim under the park single-seam vocabulary. The two systems still meet only in the existing terminal and displacement takes.
  - S3-Q2 Displacement-settles-first invariant — still needed after fusion? Adopted: yes, as safety net inside the table. Closing the insert-to-attach window removes the reachable trigger, but the identity check stays as the outcome branch that proves append-to-wrong-row impossible, per the same-identity exchange precedent.
  - S3-Q3 Exactly-once delivery — how is it preserved when the waiter is born with the row? Adopted: appended once inside the single transition, delivered once via the untouched terminal take or displacement take, both outside the lock. No new delivery path, so no double-delivery or dropped-delivery shape is introduced.
  - S3-Q4 Retry preservation — does birth-attach need a settlement rule? Adopted: no. The existing retry transition already preserves row waiters in place; birth-attach rides it with zero special-casing, pinned by the existing waiter-across-retry tests.
  - S3-Q5 What does S1 change first that forces sequencing? Adopted: S1 owns the enqueue funnel spelling (exchange call plus displaced delivery plus stale-evict order). S3 extends that seam with waiter-attach rather than re-spelling it; writing S3 first would fork the funnel both tickets must edit.
  - S3-Q6 One ticket or three? Adopted: three (fuse beside old, migrate entry, pin behavior) as expand–contract, so each lands green and the standalone-attach deletion is independently reviewable.
  - Rejected: fusing the park waiter into the same seam (mixes capability-wait with result-rendezvous and reopens the park fuse scope), deleting the settled-early branch as dead (loses the safety-net proof; the cancel-before-attach pin needs a branch to target), editing the settlement decision to know about continuations (reopens single-settlement for zero gain).
- Deletion test is the acceptance bar: deleting the standalone attach-after-enqueue path from the entry (callers read only the fused rendezvous seam) changes nothing observable through the public seam.
- Source of prototype decisions: none — no prototype; decisions derive from the two-crossing plus wedged-gap inventory and single-transition ownership decisions.
