## Problem Statement

The lifecycle table owns two same-identity replace paths that spell the same four-step protocol: look up the non-terminal row for the identity, displace-plus-terminalize the occupant to expired, insert the new pending row, and produce the stale slot-evict obligation. The fire-and-forget entry crosses one seam for this while the schedule-and-wait entry crosses a second seam that repeats the displacement spelling and adds continuation-waiter attach plus a settled-early branch. A reviewer asking "what does same-identity replace do?" must hold both spellings plus the wedged insert-to-attach gap together: between insert and attach, a cancel or a same-identity reschedule can terminalize the fresh row while its waiter is still homeless, and only the second seam's identity check keeps that waiter from hanging or joining another Lease's delivery list. Each seam is correct in isolation; together they force every displacement audit to re-prove the same ordering twice, every waiter audit to re-prove the same identity guard twice, and any future fix to the replace protocol to land in two places or drift. No behavior bug is alleged — overwrite expires the old Lease exactly once, sequential reuse after terminal inserts cleanly, stale slot waiters evict after settle — but the same-identity exchange carries duplicated depth: one replace reviewed as two protocols with two test matrices.

## Solution

Fuse same-identity displacement into one table-owned exchange primitive parameterized for both scheduling entries: a single locked transition takes the new Lease intent plus its execution plus its Tasks waiter list, atomically displaces any non-terminal occupant for the same identity to expired and installs the new pending row already carrying its waiters, returning the displaced delivery payload plus the single stale slot-evict obligation. The two existing table entries become thin adapters over that core — the fire-and-forget adapter passing its completion-waiter list, the schedule-and-wait adapter wrapping its single continuation waiter as a one-element list and mapping the core outcome to its parked-vs-settled-early shape. Both scheduling entries keep their current call shapes, so the public scheduling seam and the caller-side delivery order are untouched. No behavior change except atomicity and de-duplication: the insert-to-attach window closes inside the core, the identity check moves from adapter patch to core outcome, and the two same-identity test matrices collapse to one.

## User Stories

1. As a Tasks maintainer, I want one exchange primitive for same-identity replace, so that I stop auditing two displacement spellings to answer one replace question.
2. As a concurrent scheduler, I want no half-displaced identity window on either entry, so that a lookup between displace and insert never sees a stale row or a missing row.
3. As a waiting caller, I want my continuation waiter parked in the same locked transition that inserts my row, so that no cancel or reschedule racing the gap can ever find my waiter homeless.
4. As a displaced Lease, I want exactly-once terminal delivery with waiter preservation, so that schedule-and-wait holders of the old Lease resume with expiry rather than hanging or joining the new Lease.
5. As a new Lease, I want a clean pending row carrying only my own waiters, so that the old Lease's waiters never leak into my delivery list.
6. As a cancelled waiter, I want cancel racing my attach to deliver me exactly once through the normal row path, so that the outcome no longer depends on which side of the gap the cancel lands.
7. As a slot operator, I want settled-then-evicted-then-inserted observable order preserved with the same owner-scoped stale match, so that the new Lease's queued waiter survives while the old Lease's stale waiter is removed exactly as today.
8. As a slot FIFO waiter, I want every displacement to drain its stale head entry, so that no stranded head blocks admission after the holder releases.
9. As a cancel caller, I want cancel-before-exchange and cancel-after-exchange to both land on the right Lease, so that no cancel is lost across the fused primitive.
10. As a park reviewer, I want displaced park state taken and cancelled exactly once outside the lock, so that no parked waiter strands when its row is displaced and the park single-seam vocabulary needs no re-audit.
11. As a retry integrator, I want retry re-queue through the same Lease identity untouched with waiters preserved in place, so that completions and live Bridge views survive a retry exactly as today.
12. As a priority scheduler, I want order and counts to agree through the single core, so that the immutable snapshot reads exactly as the two sequential paths produced.
13. As a test author, I want displacement proven by behavior through the public schedule/cancel/wait seam, so that the fuse is verified by outcomes not by primitive spelling — except one mechanical check proving only one displacement protocol remains in the table.
14. As a test author, I want one same-identity test matrix instead of two, so that overwrite, cancel-during-overwrite, and waiter-guard pins each exist once and cover both entries.
15. As a future contributor, I want the only way to displace to also carry the waiters and the evict obligation, so that no second exchange-without-evict or insert-without-attach protocol can be reintroduced by a new path.
16. As a D4 integrator, I want both scheduling entries' call shapes stable through this work, so that kickPump-adjacent changes land without rebasing around a parallel caller rewrite.
17. As an ADR reader, I want the change traceable to single-settlement, unified-lifecycle-ownership, single scoped-hold, single rendezvous with outcome preservation, and same-identity policy decisions, so that the fusion clearly respects prior decisions rather than reopening them.
18. As a Bridge consumer, I want wait-then-run delivery and timeout behavior identical before and after, so that this refactor stays invisible from Objective-C.

## Implementation Decisions

- **Module**: Tasks remains the deep module behind the schedule/cancel/pending/active seam. The lifecycle table gains one parameterized exchange core (new-Lease intent plus execution plus Tasks waiter list in, displaced delivery payload plus stale slot-evict obligation out); the two existing table entries become thin adapters over it. No new module is introduced.
- **Interface**: the public scheduling seam is unchanged (schedule/schedule-and-wait/cancel/pending/active plus the result rendezvous). The internal change is two displacement spellings collapse into one core: the fire-and-forget adapter passes its completion-waiter list through, the schedule-and-wait adapter wraps its single continuation waiter as a one-element list and maps the core outcome to parked-vs-settled-early. Both scheduling entries keep their current call shapes.
- **Adapter**: the schedule-and-wait adapter owns only outcome mapping — parked pumps the pending path, settled-early resumes immediately with the returned settled Lease without pumping and without attaching elsewhere — plus the existing result shaping (completed resumes with the typed value, every other terminal resumes nil). No identity-guard or settled-early patch logic remains duplicated in the adapter; the check lives once in the core.
- **Settlement ownership**: the single terminal decision stays the only decider and is not edited. The core terminalizes the displaced Lease together with its row removal inside the one locked transition (Lease mutation plus row write together, per single-writer discipline) and returns the delivery payload; callers deliver waiters, cancel the taken park waiter, and release via the existing scope-exit path outside the lock. No second terminal path is introduced.
- **Evict ordering**: settled-then-evicted-then-inserted observable order is preserved as (settled-plus-inserted atomically)-then-evicted. The slot stale-evict stays a separate lock step after the table transition under its own lock with the same owner-scoped stale match keyed by the newcomer identity, never nested. The obligation is present exactly when a non-terminal occupant was displaced and absent otherwise, shared by both adapters.
- **Lease-identity guard**: the identity check lives once inside the core locked step. A mismatched row provably returns the settled Lease and never appends to another Lease's waiter list; the settled-early branch stays as a safety net even though birth-attach fusion closes the gap that exercises it today, mirroring the exchange precedent.
- **Wedged-continuation safety**: the continuation waiter closure must not suspend under lock; continuation resume, waiter delivery, park-waiter cancellation, slot evict, expiry race, registry wait, and slot hold stay outside the locked section exactly as today.
- **Park interplay**: the capability-wait task system is explicitly out of the fused core. Parked-state take-and-cancel on displacement, wake-clear, and terminal-take-and-cancel keep the park single-seam vocabulary verbatim; the continuation waiter joins the row waiter list, never the park waiter slot.
- **Order and counts**: the core removes the old identity from order and moves its place to removed, then enqueues the new identity at its priority and moves nil to pending, so the immutable snapshot agrees exactly as the sequential paths produced. Terminal rows need no settle: sequential reuse after terminal inserts without a displaced payload, identical to today.
- **Depth**: depth decreases by one caller-visible protocol (two displacement spellings to one core plus two thin adapters). No new abstraction layer is added; the core is the same row write plus Lease liveness check already owned by the table, with the waiter list parameterized, not a new displacement layer.
- **Seam**: the highest seam stays the Tasks schedule/cancel/pending/active facade plus the single result rendezvous and the registry whole-set wait. Tests cross that seam; no new seam is created. The lifecycle table remains the single writer.
- **Expand–contract**: add the parameterized core beside the two existing spellings, migrate both adapters onto it, then delete the duplicated lookup-displace-insert spelling so only the core performs displacement. Each ticket lands green because the old form still compiles until the migrate ticket lands.
- **Locality**: displace plus insert plus waiter-attach plus order plus counts plus the identity check share one reviewable block with the single transition, so the same-identity policy needs no hop into either adapter to audit.
- **Leverage**: leverage is drift removal plus window closure plus matrix collapse: one protocol to audit, one owner of waiter lifetime from birth, zero adapter-side ordering comments to keep in sync, one pin suite instead of two. Risk is bounded because the move is spelling-plus-ordering only — same rows, same places, same snapshot derivation, same waiter identity and delivery — only the duplication and the interleaving window go away.
- **Collision avoidance**: implementers touch only table methods; both scheduling entries' call shapes stay stable so kickPump-adjacent work in the same file never shares an edit hunk with this fusion.
- **Respects ADRs**: single-settlement path constrains the displaced outcome to expired with exactly-once delivery plus waiter preservation and scope-exit release untouched; unified lifecycle ownership requires the table (not either caller) to own the row transition plus waiter lifetime; single scoped-hold discipline forbids moving slot admission or release into the core; single rendezvous with outcome preservation requires every wait to stay on the one identity-guarded path with waiter preservation across retry; same-identity policy stays (displacement settles old, new takes identity; sequential reuse after terminal keeps working).

## Testing Decisions

- A good test crosses the public Tasks seam (schedule/schedule-and-wait/cancel/pending/active) and asserts externally observable behavior (concurrent same-identity schedules displace exactly once with old waiters delivered expired and new Lease pending; wait resumes with the value on success and nil on every other terminal; cancel of a pending wait delivers nil exactly once; same-identity displacement of an in-flight wait expires the old waiter exactly once while the new Lease completes; retry preserves the waiter to recovery; stale slot waiter evicted with newcomer surviving and no evict on non-displaced enqueue; order and counts agree; counts drain to zero), never the spelling of the core — except one mechanical check proving only one displacement protocol remains in the table.
- Modules under test: lifecycle table exchange core (non-terminal displaced with payload versus terminal-or-missing with no payload, fresh identity parks waiters, mismatched identity returns settled without appending elsewhere, order plus counts agreement, parked payload taken), both adapters (fire-and-forget displaced-then-evict-then-deliver order; schedule-and-wait parked pumps while settled-early resumes without pumping), enqueue funnel (stale drained once per displacement, zero evicts otherwise, settle-first ordering intact).
- Prior art: same-identity suites (displacement settles old expired, waiter exactly-once, sequential reuse after terminal), schedule-and-wait rendezvous suites (waiter preserved across retry, completed/failed exactly-once, overwrite expires old once with new completing), lifecycle suites (terminal removal plus counts, double-park plus cancel-while-parked), determinism suites (virtual-time timeout, cancel-pins-expired, priority high-before-low, slot serialization), slot-hold suites (admission refusal, FIFO wake, cancellable wait, stale-evicts plus self-evicts plus no-op guards). The fusion must keep every one green with zero test-semantics edits except new pins added beside them and the deliberate matrix collapse.
- Verification per ticket: `swift build` green plus full `swift test` green; the duplicated displacement spelling shrinks monotonically (two protocols beside the new core after fuse, one core only after migrate) and the two same-identity matrices converge to one after the pin ticket.

## Out of Scope

- Any change to lifecycle reachability (which states are legal from pending vs parked vs active), priority ordering, retry budget or void-completion policy, waiter preservation or exactly-once delivery semantics, slot limits or release ownership (stays with activation scope-exit), expiry resolution (task timeout vs configured default), or time adapters.
- Changing the public Lease shape, the typed-optional wait signatures, the Bridge live-view reflection, State creation/observation, registry emission order or lock discipline, generation prune or shared-slot coupling, or deterministic-time spelling.
- Touching the slot evict primitive itself, the park capability-waiter primitive, Deadline internals, the registry wait implementation, the pump/kick discipline, or the activation gate beyond its existing call site.
- Reopening single-settlement, unified-lifecycle-ownership, scoped-hold, Lease-keyed hold, single-rendezvous, or same-identity policy decisions: no revisit trigger is met; the fusion extends them to one parameterized core, it does not redesign them.
- Reformatting or renames beyond the fusion.

## Further Notes

- Dependency context: D2 has no blockers and blocks nothing; it sits in the independent group. Implementers constrain edits to table methods only and keep both scheduling entries' call shapes stable, so D4's kickPump work in the same file never collides. Sequenced against the post-exchange, post-rendezvous, post-evict-pairing state: the fused core preserves the obligation-carrying result and the birth-attach rendezvous rather than re-spelling either.
- Self-grill verdict (grill-with-docs, autonomous self-grill; decided, no questions asked):
  - D2-Q1 Core parameter shape: waiter-list param versus continuation-specific param? Adopted waiter list. The row waiter list is the canonical Tasks waiter store; the schedule-and-wait adapter wraps its single continuation as a one-element list. A continuation-typed core would fork the waiter vocabulary a second time.
  - D2-Q2 Settled-early ownership: core outcome versus adapter patch? Adopted core outcome with thin adapter mapping. The identity check and the parked-vs-settled distinction live once in the locked step; adapters only map to their existing return shapes. Keeping the check in the adapter would preserve the exact duplication under review.
  - D2-Q3 Caller stability versus caller migration: Adopted stability. Both scheduling entries keep their current call shapes and only the table internals fuse, so D4's pump work never shares a hunk with this fusion. Migrating callers to a single call site would couple two independent candidates for zero behavioral gain.
  - D2-Q4 Insert-to-attach window: separate attach step versus birth-attach in core? Adopted birth-attach. The waiter list enters in the same locked transition as the row insert, so cancel racing attach resolves through the normal row path and the settled-early branch becomes an unreachable safety net rather than the primary guard.
  - D2-Q5 Evict obligation shape: one shared shape versus per-adapter shapes? Adopted one shared obligation (present exactly on non-terminal displacement) drained by the existing funnel step. Per-adapter shapes would re-split the pairing the evict-pairing work just unified.
  - D2-Q6 Ticket count: one versus three? Adopted three (parameterize core beside old, collapse duplicates onto core, collapse test matrices) as expand–contract, so each lands green and the duplicate-deletion plus matrix-collapse are independently reviewable.
  - Rejected: fusing the slot evict into the core (nests lock domains and violates Store-to-Tasks order), merging the row-waiter list with the park waiter slot (reopens the park fuse scope), changing the displaced outcome from expired (reopens the Lease contract), touching the pump/kick discipline (belongs to D4).
- Deletion test is the acceptance bar: deleting the duplicated lookup-displace-insert spelling from the table (both adapters read only the parameterized core) changes nothing observable through the public seam.
- Source of prototype decisions: none — no prototype; decisions derive from the two-seam displacement inventory, the wedged-gap analysis, and the single-transition ownership decisions.
