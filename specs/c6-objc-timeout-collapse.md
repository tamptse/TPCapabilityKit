## Problem Statement

The Bridge timeout fork resolves in one place but reads in three: the mapper-internal timeout form owns the wire-negative vs explicit fork plus a display fallback and an explicitness check, the mapper re-exposes display plus explicitness plus descriptor construction as three readers, and the descriptor exposes a collapsed timeout reading plus an explicitness flag as the two `@objc` observables. On top of that, the two value-returning wait entries build descriptors on different paths: schedule-and-wait takes a caller-built descriptor while run-when-available builds one inline via the mapper with hardcoded priority and retry values. Readers reviewing the ObjC timeout contract (ADR-0020) currently hop between the resolver, the three mapper readers, the two descriptor observables, and the two scheduler build paths to confirm there is still only one policy.

## Solution

Collapse to one policy with two observable cases and one descriptor-building core, with no expiry semantics change: keep the single wire resolver as the sole statement of the fork (wire-negative means unspecified, every non-negative wire travels as explicit, the omitted call arrives as the pinned compat literal collapsing to explicit per ADR-0020); store the enum form once in the descriptor so the two `@objc` observables (collapsed display reading vs explicitness flag) read from one stored match instead of two independent derivations; route run-when-available through the same descriptor-building core as the descriptor entries so both wait paths share one build plus the one delivery core; consolidate duplicated `@objc` defaults into one private construction core without changing any `@objc` signature. The C5 expiry contract stays untouched: Swift `nil` still resolves to the configured default once at Deadline construction, the pinned literal never follows a reconfigured default, and the single race stays the only expiry seam.

## User Stories

1. As an ObjC consumer, I want an omitted timeout to keep reading as the pinned compat literal, so that my existing calls behave exactly as before.
2. As an ObjC consumer, I want a negative timeout to mean unspecified, so that the scheduler configured default applies at execution.
3. As an ObjC consumer, I want an explicit timeout (including one equal to the pin literal) to travel unchanged, so that my chosen value is never reinterpreted.
4. As an ObjC consumer, I want the collapsed timeout reading plus the explicitness flag to tell the two cases apart, so that display parity never confuses unspecified with explicit.
5. As a scheduler maintainer, I want one resolver to state the whole fork once, so that the next timeout tweak touches one place.
6. As a scheduler maintainer, I want both wait entries to share one descriptor-building core plus one delivery core, so that run-when-available cannot drift from schedule-and-wait.
7. As a scheduler maintainer, I want one defaults source behind both descriptor entries, so that the auto-id and client-id paths cannot diverge on priority, timeout, retry, or metadata defaults.
8. As a reviewer, I want the descriptor to store the enum and expose exactly two observable cases, so that no third omitted state leaks downstream.
9. As a reviewer, I want the `@objc` surface (collapsed timeout, explicitness flag, both constructors, both wait entries) byte-for-byte in signature, so that no ObjC call-site migration is needed.
10. As a test author, I want the existing timeout suites to stay green without edits, so that the collapse is proven by pinned observable behavior rather than new seams.
11. As a Settlement reviewer, I want Deadline construction plus the single race untouched, so that the C5 expiry guarantees (resolve-once, won-gated read, stored-nil vs never-stored) read the same as before.
12. As a future deepener, I want policy-once plus readers-thin plus build-once locality, so that the next Bridge change lands in the mapper core, not across three readers and two build paths.

## Implementation Decisions

- **Module**: the mapper module keeps owning the whole ObjC timeout fork behind its interface; the Bridge view and both descriptor creation paths stay thin translators. No new module is introduced; no logic moves into the view or the scheduler entry.
- **Interface**: the single wire resolver stays the sole policy statement (wire-negative to unspecified, non-negative to explicit, omitted pin collapsing to explicit). The stored `resolved` reading, the collapsed display reading, and the explicitness check become three thin readers over one stored enum match — no reader restates the fork condition independently.
- **Depth**: no depth change — the collapse removes duplication without adding abstraction. The descriptor keeps wrapping the Swift descriptor as its domain payload while additionally holding the resolved enum as its stored timeout source; downstream (Deadline construction, lease payload) keeps reading the optional form only.
- **Seam**: the highest seams stay where they are. Tests keep crossing the descriptor observable seam (collapsed reading plus explicitness flag) and the wait-delivery seam (schedule-and-wait vs run-when-available through the one delivery core). No new seam is created; the inline mapper build inside run-when-available is removed in favor of the shared descriptor-building core.
- **Adapter**: no adapter change beyond internal consolidation. The Bridge remains the single ObjC scheduling adapter; completion delivery (given queue or main) and the sync fast-path (check-and-run bypassing Lease, slot admission, and Deadline by contract) are untouched.
- **Leverage**: leverage the existing timeout suites as the verification seam — the resolver suite (omitted-pin, wire-negative, explicit preserved, both-paths-share-resolver), the display-collapse suite, the timeout-contract suite (Swift nil vs omitted pin vs negative at Deadline construction), and the timeout-pins suite (configured default applies to waiter). No new test seam is needed for the collapse itself.
- **Locality**: policy lives once in the mapper core; both observables read adjacent to the stored enum; both wait entries read adjacent to the shared build-plus-delivery core; both `@objc` constructors delegate adjacent to the single defaults core. A reader confirms the fork without leaving the mapper interface.
- **Stored form**: the descriptor stores the resolved enum once at construction; the collapsed display and the explicitness flag are two views over that single stored match (explicit carries the value, unspecified falls back to the pinned literal for display and reads false for explicitness). Re-deriving either view from the optional payload with an independent condition is rejected because the two views could drift.
- **Build-path unification**: run-when-available stops building via a direct mapper call with inline priority and retry literals and instead crosses the same descriptor-building core as the descriptor entries (same capability mapping, same priority coercion, same retry and metadata defaults), then shares the one wait-then-run delivery core with schedule-and-wait. The Strong-safe condition is that the unified path preserves the current observable triple (capability set, coerced priority, resolved timeout) exactly — verified by the wait-then-run resolver-sharing test.
- **`@objc` surface care**: the collapsed timeout property, the explicitness flag, both descriptor constructors, and both wait entries keep their signatures verbatim. Internal consolidation never renames, removes, or deprecates a public selector in this change; any future deprecation is a separate decision with its own migration note.
- **Defaults single source**: the auto-id and client-id constructors delegate to one private construction core that owns every default (priority, omitted-timeout pin, retry count, metadata). The two `@objc` spellings keep their current default literals in signature but forward to the core, so signature defaults and core defaults cannot diverge silently.
- **Respects ADRs**: ADR-0020 (omitted pins compat literal as explicit; wire-negative stays unspecified resolved once at Deadline construction) is the authoritative fork statement; ADR-0003/0016 (single scheduling adapter behind one live view, thin translators) constrains the build-path unification; ADR-0012 plus ADR-0023 (single race for wait and execution, timeout resolved once, adapters behind one sleep seam) forbid any expiry-ward drift.
- **Respects C5**: the C5 expiry contract is frozen — three-layer Deadline to Clock to VirtualTime depth, `race(operation:)` untouched, won-gated read untouched, Settlement decision table untouched, registry wait seam untouched.

## Testing Decisions

- A good test pins externally observable timeout behavior (omitted reads as the pin and flags explicit, negative stores nil and flags non-explicit while displaying the pin, explicit travels unchanged, both constructors agree, run-when-available agrees with schedule-and-wait) through the descriptor observables plus the deterministic clock, never the stored enum shape or lock internals.
- Modules under test: descriptor observables (both constructors, live-view wrap of a Swift-nil descriptor, client-id identity), mapper resolver (omitted vs negative vs explicit, both-paths-share), wait entries (negative-unspecified and explicit through run-when-available), Deadline construction (Swift nil vs omitted pin vs negative against a non-default configured default). Prior art: the ObjC mapper suite, the timeout-contract suite, the timeout-pins suite, and the single-view suite are the models for any touched test.
- Verification for the collapse: full `swift test` green with zero test edits; the resolver, display-collapse, contract, and pins suites must pass unchanged, proving the omitted-vs-negative-vs-explicit triple and the Deadline resolve-once behavior survived. Any suite naming the removed duplicate condition fails the ticket.
- Verification for the build-path unification: the wait-then-run resolver-sharing test plus the descriptor-construction-agreement tests pass unchanged; a new thin assertion that run-when-available builds through the shared core (same priority coercion, same timeout triple) is acceptable only if it crosses the existing descriptor-observable seam.

## Out of Scope

- Any change to timeout resolution (task value vs configured default), to the pinned-compat literal value, or to the wire-negative-means-unspecified rule.
- Any change to Deadline construction, the single race, the sleep seam, the Settlement decision table, retry budget, void policy, or slot-release ownership.
- Any change to the registry wait interface, park mechanics, activation gates, generation sharing, or whole-set readiness.
- Any `@objc` signature rename, removal, or deprecation; any Swift-side `TaskDescriptor` shape change.
- Collapsing the time module into Deadline, threading time per call, or forking virtual time per generation.

## Further Notes

- Grill outcome (grill-with-docs, autonomous): Worth, build-path merge Strong-safe. Cut-now alternatives were challenged — (a) deleting the explicitness flag in favor of display-only was rejected because display collapses unspecified and explicit-pin to the same literal and reviewers would lose the only disambiguator; (b) letting run-when-available keep its inline build was rejected because hardcoded priority and retry literals already duplicate the descriptor defaults and will drift; (c) restating the fork inside each reader for "readability" was rejected because three independent conditions triple the edit surface for the next timeout change. The load-bearing constraints are: single resolver owns the `< 0` condition once, both observables read one stored match, the unified build preserves the observable triple exactly, and the `@objc` surface stays signature-frozen.
- Vocabulary: module (mapper timeout detail, Bridge view, Tasks path), interface (resolver seam, descriptor-observable seam, wait-delivery seam), depth (no new layer), seam (single resolver, single build core, single delivery core, single race untouched), adapter (live vs virtual behind one sleep seam, Bridge as single ObjC adapter), leverage (existing resolver plus contract plus pins suites as the verification seam), locality (policy once, readers adjacent, build once).
- Source of decisions: no prototype; decisions derive from ADR-0020 (plus R8 follow-up), the current Bridge files (mapper resolver plus three readers, descriptor with two constructors plus two observables, scheduler with dual build paths into one delivery core), and the frozen C5 spec (specs/c5-expiry-box-naming.md).
