## Problem Statement

Deterministic-time control is spelled three times for one virtual advance: the Store facade owns a trio of thin adapters with a fresh-instance precondition, the scheduling generations re-spell the same three controls plus the precede-first-use gate next to the pruned count, and the time module re-spells them a third time as adapter enable plus advance plus waiter rendezvous with the virtual registry policy underneath. A reviewer asking "what must hold before I advance virtual time?" must hop facade precondition, generations gate, adapter gate, and the implied waiter-registration plus poll/yield timing chain to assemble one answer, and each hop is a drift risk: a fourth spelling of the fresh-instance rule, a gate that checks the wrong count, or a timing-policy tweak applied on one side only. The sum-vs-share split (snapshot sums pending/active across live generations while slot admission shares one Store-owned domain so reconfigure never doubles the configured limit during drain) is stated once as prose but enforced across three modules, so a future reader cannot tell which seam owns the invariant.

## Solution

Keep the shared slot-domain plus shared-clock wiring exactly as it is but delete one delegation layer: expose the deterministic controls on the scheduling generations only, with the Store facade keeping production scheduling while tests cross the narrower generations seam instead of the facade for time. The fresh-instance rule and the precede-first-use rule collapse to one adjacent gate in generations, the time module stays a pure live/virtual adapter, and the sum-vs-share split is promoted from prose to a single snapshot postcondition test. No behavior change: enable-once, virtual-only advance, waiter rendezvous, shared slot domain, shared clock across generations, and prune-then-loop snapshot arithmetic are identical before and after. This spec fires the ADR revisit on prune-stable grounds and sequences the work expand-contract so the suite stays green throughout.

## User Stories

1. As a scheduler maintainer, I want deterministic-time controls spelled in one place instead of three, so that enable/advance/wait reviews in a single hop.
2. As a test author, I want to drive virtual time through the narrower generations seam, so that tests never touch the time adapter directly and the facade carries no time spelling.
3. As a Store reviewer, I want the facade to keep only production scheduling (configure, schedule, schedule-and-wait, cancel, pending, active), so that production traffic and deterministic-time control never share a spelling layer.
4. As a generations reviewer, I want the precede-first-use gate to live exactly once next to the pruned count it guards, so that no second gate can disagree about what "first" means.
5. As a fresh-instance reviewer, I want the never-on-shared rule stated once beside the precede-first-use gate, so that no forwarder re-spells the singleton guard.
6. As a time-module reviewer, I want the clock to stay a pure live/virtual adapter with enable-once and virtual-only gates, so that no scheduling count leaks into the adapter.
7. As a virtual-time debugger, I want the implied waiter rendezvous (await-registered plus poll/yield timing policy) stated once in the time module, so that no caller re-spells the timing policy.
8. As a reconfigure integrator, I want the shared slot domain plus shared clock wiring untouched, so that drain-while-new-work-enters keeps today's limit and time behavior.
9. As a counts consumer, I want the snapshot to keep summing pending/active across live generations in one prune-then-loop pass, so that reconfigure never drops or doubles in-flight rows.
10. As a future contributor, I want the sum-vs-share split proven by one snapshot postcondition test, so that the invariant survives even if the prose rots.
11. As a Store-lock reviewer, I want the Store-to-Tasks lock order and the pruned-snapshot discipline unchanged, so that no new lock domain appears with this trim.
12. As a facade consumer, I want the live-generation count to keep reading through the same seam as pending/active, so that no caller straddles two seams for one generation question.
13. As a cautious merger, I want the work sequenced expand-then-migrate-then-contract, so that no intermediate state breaks the suite and deletion is mechanical.
14. As an ADR reader, I want the change traceable to shared-slot coupling and time-module ownership, so that the trim clearly respects prior decisions rather than reopening them.
15. As a reviewer of the alternative, I want the gate-in-adapter option recorded with its rejection reason, so that a future attempt does not re-litigate the same trade without new evidence.

## Implementation Decisions

- **Module**: The scheduling generations become the single owner of deterministic-time spelling (enable with both gates, advance, waiter rendezvous); the Store facade loses its three time forwarders but keeps production scheduling; the time module is untouched except for caller updates. No new module is introduced; one delegation layer is deleted.
- **Interface**: The public scheduling seam is unchanged for production callers. The deterministic seam narrows from facade-level to generations-level; test-only callers cross the narrower seam. The fresh-instance precondition (never on the shared singleton) and the precede-first-use precondition (never after first schedule) collapse to one adjacent gate in generations, stated once, not per-forwarder.
- **Depth**: Depth decreases by one delegation layer (facade forwarders deleted). No new abstraction is added; the generations gate plus the pruned-count read stay adjacent in the same function, and the adapter gate stays where it is. The alternative (gate moves into the adapter, generations drop the count check) is rejected because the adapter would stop being pure and every future re-use of the time module would inherit a scheduling concept.
- **Seam**: The highest time seam becomes the generations deterministic interface. Tests cross that seam; no new seam is created and no test touches the clock or virtual registry directly. The snapshot (pending, active, live-generation count) remains the one generations read tests assert through. Expansion exposes the generations seam beside the facade first so migration batches stay green before contraction deletes the facade trio.
- **Adapter**: No adapter change. Live/virtual adapters plus the advance policy stay behind the one sleep seam in the time module; the single shared time instance across generations is preserved so reconfigure never forks virtual time.
- **Locality**: The fresh-instance check, the precede-first-use check, the pruned generation count, the enable-once swap, and the virtual-only advance/wait gates sit in two adjacent places at most (both scheduling gates beside the count in generations; adapter gates inside the clock), so the full "what must hold before advance" path reads without hopping facade to generations to clock to virtual policy.
- **Leverage**: Leverage is delegation deletion plus invariant promotion (prose to postcondition test): one gate pair to audit, one timing policy to extend, one snapshot test to guard sum-vs-share. Risk is bounded because the change is spelling-only — every forwarder body today is a single call — but blast radius is real (order of a dozen test files plus the shared fixture), which is why the work splits expand, migrate, contract.
- **Respects ADRs**: Shared slot domain across generations forbids any admission-domain change — the trim touches spelling only; time-module ownership (live/virtual adapters plus advance policy behind one sleep seam, one shared time instance, Store keeps thin spelling-only delegation — this change narrows that delegation deliberately without moving adapter policy outward) is preserved; facade-split parking (deterministic spelling stayed on the facade to avoid churning the test surface; revisit only on fourth lock domain, renewed friction, or stable prune — this spec fires on the stable-prune leg, see Further Notes); Store-to-Tasks lock order requires prune-then-loop and emission discipline to stay verbatim.

## Testing Decisions

- A good test crosses the generations deterministic seam (or the facade production seam for counts) and asserts externally observable behavior (enable before first schedule succeeds; enable after first schedule traps; enable on the shared singleton traps; advance expires exactly the due waiters once; wait-for-waiters rendezvous synchronizes without a second policy spelling; snapshot sums pending/active across two live generations during drain while admission still enforces one shared limit; reconfigure never forks virtual time), never the spelling of the deleted forwarder — except one mechanical grep proving no facade time forwarder remains.
- Modules under test: generations deterministic controls (dual gate, advance delegation, waiter rendezvous), snapshot postcondition (sum pending/active, share slot domain, prune drained, live-generation count agrees), time module untouched behavior (enable-once, virtual-only advance/wait, advance wakes only expired exactly once).
- Prior art: deterministic-time suites around enable-precedes-first-use, advance/wait rendezvous, and reconfigure-drain count suites around pending/active aggregation plus shared-limit admission; the trim must keep every one green with test-seam updates only (facade-time call sites re-pointed to generations), zero behavior-semantics edits, plus one new snapshot postcondition test promoting the sum-vs-share prose to code.

## Out of Scope

- Any change to time semantics (enable-once, virtual-only advance/wait, advance-wakes-only-expired exactly once, rendezvous timing values, non-negative advance, shared clock across generations).
- Any change to slot admission (shared Store-owned domain, scoped hold, FIFO wake, cancellable wait, scope-exit release) or snapshot arithmetic (prune-then-loop, sum across live, drain naturally).
- Touching State creation/observation, capability registry emission or whole-set wait, lifecycle/settlement, Deadline timeout-once resolution, Bridge mapping, or lease identity.
- Introducing a scheduler protocol, new public API, new lock domain, per-generation clocks, or per-generation slot isolation.
- Renames beyond the trim or formatting churn.

## Further Notes

- Grill record (solo, five-question frontier):
  - Q: "Why does the facade trio resist direct deletion?" A: Order of a dozen test files plus the shared deterministic fixture cross the facade trio today; deleting first breaks the suite with no green intermediate. Hence expand-contract: expose the generations seam beside the facade, migrate call sites in batches, then delete.
  - Q: "Why is the generations seam the narrowest correct one?" A: One level down (clock/virtual directly) leaks the rendezvous timing policy and confuses enable-once with precede-first-use; one level up (facade) mixes production scheduling with time plus the singleton guard. Generations sits beside the pruned count it guards and already owns the shared-clock wiring, so it is the narrowest seam that still guards first-use.
  - Q: "Where do the two gates land, and why not in the adapter?" A: Both the fresh-instance rule and the precede-first-use rule land once in generations enable, adjacent to the pruned-count read. Moving either into the clock makes the adapter know scheduling counts; every future re-use of the time module would inherit a generations concept. Adapter purity wins the tie-break.
  - Q: "What is the migration blast radius?" A: Enable call sites across roughly eleven test files plus the shared fixture, advance/wait call sites across roughly fifteen files (overlapping), live-generation-count reads across four files. All re-pointed mechanically to the generations seam with zero behavior-semantics edits; the fixture's deterministic flag re-points once and carries most suites.
  - Q: "What guarantees production traffic is untouched?" A: Configure, schedule, schedule-and-wait, cancel, pending, active keep their facade spelling and delegation verbatim; slot domain, shared clock, snapshot arithmetic, and lock order stay identical; deletion removes only time forwarders plus their per-forwarder singleton guards.
- Trigger (binding): this spec fires the parked-facade revisit on the stable-prune leg — prune-then-loop plus snapshot sum-vs-share settled through the reconfigure-drain cycles with the full suite green and no fourth lock domain introduced. The implementing agent must cite the green prune/snapshot evidence in the first ticket before editing code. Same-file ordering still applies: if the state-observe waiter work is unmerged, it lands first.
- Live-generation-count placement tie-break: keep the count with the counts (same seam as pending/active) rather than moving it with time, because its readers ask a generations question ("how many live?"), not a time question; only move it if the trigger review decides the whole generation-count concept belongs behind the narrower seam.
- Source of prototype decisions: none — no prototype; decisions derive from the three-hop spelling inventory (facade to generations to clock/virtual) and the shared-slot plus time-ownership decisions.
