## Problem Statement

A reviewer asking "where can a Task's place change?" must hold four writers plus one dead overload together: a standalone insert that any future caller can reach to overwrite a row, an exchange that already owns same-id displacement atomically yet delegates its new-row half to that standalone insert, a dequeue that moves pending to parked with its own inline place write, a wake that clears park waiter state with its own inline row patch, and a single transition vocabulary covering only activate, retry, and terminal. Place (pending, parked, active) plus the order index plus the incremental snapshot counts must agree on every move, yet the agreement is spread across spellings instead of stated once: insert writes rows plus order plus counts beside exchange, dequeue writes place plus counts beside transition, wake patches waiter state beside the park seam. A second, Task-accepting park overload stands uncalled beside the factory park seam, inviting a future caller to re-split the single park entry the domain already promises. Each fragment is correct in isolation; together they force every place audit to re-prove the same invariant per call site, against the single-writer discipline the domain already claims.

## Solution

Route every place change through one transition vocabulary: demote insert to a private helper callable only from exchange (the sole new-row entry), fold the dequeue pending-to-parked move and the wake waiter-clear into transition cases so no Path call site performs its own place or waiter patch, and delete the dead Task-accepting park overload keeping the factory park as the single park seam. No behavior change: same places, same priority FIFO order, same snapshot counts, same park double-park and waiter-cancel rules, same displacement atomicity, same waiter delivery. Deletion test: removing the standalone insert entry and the dead park overload (callers read only exchange plus the factory park plus transition) changes nothing observable through the public scheduling seam.

## Implementation Decisions

- **Module**: Tasks remains the deep module behind the schedule/cancel/pending/active seam. The lifecycle table keeps its shape (rows keyed by task id, internal order index, incremental snapshot); only the entry points consolidate. No new module is introduced.
- **Interface**: the public scheduling seam is unchanged (schedule/schedule-and-wait/cancel/pending/active plus result rendezvous). The internal change is entries shrink: insert becomes private and reachable only from exchange; dequeue-to-parked and wake-clear become transition cases; the Task-accepting park overload is deleted leaving the factory park as the single park entry.
- **Insert demotion**: insert performs row creation with no existing row to match on an identity guard, so it cannot be a transition case (transition requires an existing row plus Lease identity match); private-helper-of-exchange is its correct final shape, not a transition case. Exchange remains the sole new-row entry with displacement atomicity, settled-then-evicted ordering, and outside-lock delivery unchanged.
- **Dequeue fold**: the dequeue pending-to-parked move (order pop plus place write plus counts update) becomes a transition case with identical priority FIFO order and identical skip-missing-row behavior; the order index stays private mechanics with no separately callable dequeue seam.
- **Wake fold**: wake clearing (waiting flag plus waiter reset on identity match) becomes a transition case; terminal still takes the row, so take removes and no double-clear with wake exists. Park itself stays out of transition: the factory park keeps its fused shape (intent plus factory in, parked vs refused vs already-terminal out) as the single park seam.
- **Park seam preservation**: only the dead Task-form overload is deleted; the factory form is untouched including the non-suspending factory constraint under the single lock. No re-litigation of the fused park shape.
- **Counts writer**: the incremental snapshot helper stays the single counts primitive, called only from table-internal row writes (the insert helper, transition including the new cases, and the private row-take helper). No caller outside the table ever adjusts counts.
- **Lock discipline**: every consolidated transition holds the one scheduler lock once and never nests. Waiter factory invocation stays non-suspending under lock; slot evict, waiter delivery, waiter-task cancellation, expiry race, registry wait, and slot hold stay outside the locked section exactly as today.
- **Locality**: place plus order plus counts plus waiter lifetime share one reviewable transition block, so the place invariant needs no hop across insert, dequeue, wake, and transition to audit.
- **Depth**: depth decreases by two caller-visible entries (standalone insert, dead park overload) and two inline Path-side patches (dequeue place write, wake row patch) collapse into transition cases. No new abstraction is added.
- **Seam**: the highest seam stays the Tasks schedule/cancel/pending/active facade plus the single result rendezvous and the registry whole-set wait. Tests cross that seam; no new seam is created. The lifecycle table remains the single writer: each Lease mutation happens together with its row write in one locked transition.
- **Expand–contract**: demote-plus-delete lands first beside the untouched transition (mechanical, independently reviewable), then the dequeue/wake fold migrates the Path call sites, then behavior is pinned. Each ticket lands green because no ticket changes reachability, order, counts, or delivery.
- **Leverage**: leverage is audit locality plus drift removal: one vocabulary for every place change, one park entry, zero caller-side row patches. Risk is bounded because the move is spelling-only — same rows, same places, same snapshot derivation, same waiter identity — only the entry count changes.
- **Respects ADRs**: unified lifecycle ownership requires the table (not Path call sites) to own every row transition plus waiter lifetime; single counts snapshot requires place, order, and counts to keep agreeing through the one vocabulary; same-id policy (displacement settles old expired, sequential reuse after terminal untouched) is preserved verbatim; the single park-seam contract is closed toward conformance, not redesigned.

## Testing Decisions

- A good test crosses the public Tasks seam and asserts externally observable behavior (parked reachability with counts agreeing, double-schedule displacement with exactly-once delivery, cancel-while-parked landing expired with one delivery, wake clearing so re-park behaves, terminal removal cleaning order with same-id reschedule, retry restoring pending), never the spelling of the transition cases or the visibility of the insert helper — except mechanical greps proving the standalone insert entry and the dead park overload are gone and no Path call site writes place or waiter state outside transition.
- Modules under test: lifecycle table transitions (insert-via-exchange, dequeue-to-parked, wake-clear, park factory outcomes, activate/retry/terminal with counts agreement), enqueue flow (exchange atomicity with identity-guard safety net intact), Path flow (process/park/wait/activate/settle as one path through the unified vocabulary).
- Prior art: lifecycle table suites (parked reachability plus counts, double-schedule single row, cancel-while-parked, wake-clears, terminal removal plus same-id reschedule, retry restores pending), same-id exchange pins (displacement settles old expired, waiter exactly-once, sequential reuse after terminal), lifecycle transition suites, path locality proof (park/wait/cancel/retry single-delivery plus freed slot), determinism suites (virtual-time timeout, cancel-pins-expired, priority high-before-low, slot serialization), slot-hold suites (admission refusal, FIFO wake, cancellable wait). The unification must keep every one green with zero test-semantics edits.
- Verification per ticket: `swift build` green plus full `swift test` green; grep proves the old entries shrink monotonically (standalone insert plus dead overload present before, absent after; Path-side place writes present before, routed through transition after).

## Out of Scope

- Any change to lifecycle reachability (which states are legal from pending vs parked vs active), priority ordering, retry budget or void-completion policy, waiter preservation or exactly-once delivery semantics, slot limits or release ownership (stays with activation scope-exit), expiry resolution (task timeout vs configured default), or time adapters.
- Changing the public Lease shape, the Bridge live-view reflection, State creation/observation, registry emission order or lock discipline, generation prune or shared-slot coupling, or deterministic-time spelling.
- Touching the slot evict primitive itself, Deadline internals, the registry wait implementation, or the activation gate beyond its existing Path call site.
- Reopening single-settlement, unified-lifecycle-ownership, scoped-hold, same-id policy, or single park-seam decisions: no revisit trigger is met; the unification extends them to every place change, it does not redesign them.
- Reformatting or renames beyond the unification.

## Further Notes

- Grill verdict (grill-with-docs, autonomous self-grill; decided, no questions asked):
  - S1-Q1 Insert as transition case versus private helper of exchange? Adopted private helper. Transition requires an existing row plus Lease identity match; insert creates the row, so a transition-case shape would need a match-nothing special case that weakens the identity guard for zero gain. Strong angle, deepened into Implementation Decisions.
  - S1-Q2 Park into transition versus keep the factory seam? Adopted keep the factory park as the single park seam, delete only the dead Task-form overload. The factory-under-transition shape already is one locked row write; folding it into the transition enum would re-litigate the park fuse and widen the enum with a factory-carrying case for no atomicity gain.
  - S1-Q3 Dequeue into transition — does the order pop belong in the transition? Adopted yes. The pop plus place write plus counts update are one atomic step today only by caller discipline under one lock acquisition; stating them as one transition case makes the atomicity structural while the order index stays internal mechanics.
  - S1-Q4 Wake as transition case — place-neutral, so why move it? Adopted move it. Wake patches waiter state on the same row transition guards; leaving it as an inline patch keeps a second row-write spelling beside transition. Terminal-take still removes the row, so no double-clear is introduced.
  - S1-Q5 One ticket or three? Adopted three (demote-plus-delete, fold dequeue/wake, verify) in expand–contract order, so the mechanical deletion lands independently reviewable from the transition fold and each lands green.
  - S1-Q6 Sequencing vs S3/S5? Adopted S1 first: it changes spelling only, so S3/S5 build on one place vocabulary instead of auditing four writers mid-work.
  - Rejected: fusing slot evict into the transition (nests lock domains), adding a place enum beyond the existing vocabulary (leverage ~nil), changing the displaced outcome or park reachability (reopens settled contracts).
- Deletion test is the acceptance bar: deleting the standalone insert entry and the dead park overload (callers read only exchange plus the factory park plus transition) changes nothing observable through the public seam.
- Source of prototype decisions: none — no prototype; decisions derive from the four-writer plus dead-overload inventory and single-transition ownership decisions.
